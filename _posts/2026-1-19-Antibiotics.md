While reviewing companies with ambitions to develop antibiotics that address AMR, a Japanese company called **Shionogi** caught my attention.

Its antibiotics **Fetroja** has gained strong commercial momentum in the USA and EU, with a price of around $6k for a two-week treatment cycle – a premium product vs. standard of care for sure. Interestingly, the product was firstly launched in the USA in 2019 to leverage the larger market, FDA’s prioritization, and overall greater willingness among health professionals to try new solutions. It was subsequently launched in EU and Japan.

As mentioned in the last post, a biotech relying soly on antibiotics assets can rarely survive.  Shionogi, however, is not a biotech but a mature pharma company dating back to 1878, and by no means a pure play in antibiotics. 

It has **three** main revenue pillars: nearly 50% of sales from its **HIV franchise** with ViiV (a joint venture with GSK), 35% from domestic prescription drugs-especially **COVID-related** respiratory virus treatment-and about10% from overseas sales of **Fetroja**. 

The existing business model, which combines monetizing niche HIV products through royalties and dividends from ViiV with expanding the innovative antibiotics Fetroja overseas (including China), works well. It consistently generates operating margin above 35%. Cash flow from operating activities increased from 109bJYP in 2021 to 195bJYP in 2025. This momentum is expected to continue for a few more years. In HIV, Shionogi is expanding LAI (long acting injectable) formulation, which is gaining popularity among patient groups; for Fetroja, uptake in US and EU continues to grow, and China remains an opportunity to tap more.

However, the company faces risks, including over-reliance on HIV franchise, declining demand for Covid products, and the need for effective cost management related to ongoing R&D and overseas expansion. It also needs to think strategically about future pipeline, as patent cliffs for both HIV portfolio and Fetroja are inevitable in 5-7 years.

In response, the company’s latest release (H1 2025) highlights initiatives for long term growth, including an acquisition of Torii Pharma to enter the allergy and dermatology markets, as well as further investment in QOL (quality of life) assets such as insomnia and depression treatment. 

The landscape of Shionogi’s pipeline will shift from infection to QOL diseases. The goal is to build up QOL portfolio to a revenue level comparable to its Infectious Diseases segment. **The shift in strategy further underscores how challenging it is for the infectious diseases field, particularly antibiotics, to serve as a long‑term growth engine on its own.**
