<p class="p-dek">Four stories from the first week of September that matter more than their headlines suggested: a lupus trial that failed everywhere except one subgroup, Pfizer quietly moving another Seagen asset off its books, a two-weekly GLP-1 heading to Europe, and the first filing for a drug that treats the cause of Huntington's rather than its symptoms.</p>

<h2>Clinical trials</h2>

<div class="p-item">
  <p class="p-head">Alumis — <span class="p-asset">envudeucitinib (LUMUS, Phase 2b)</span><span class="p-tag">Immunology</span></p>
  <p>The 408-patient, 48-week trial missed its primary endpoint and every secondary endpoint across the overall population. Only the prespecified subgroup with a high interferon gene signature responded, and Alumis will take a Phase 3 confined to that subgroup to regulators. Shares fell about 55%. The separate psoriasis filing is still guided for the fourth quarter.</p>
  <p>The wider read is about the drug class. TYK2 inhibitors have worked well in psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis and have now disappointed repeatedly outside them — this result adds to that pattern rather than breaking it.</p>
  <p class="p-ind"><b>The disease</b> Systemic lupus erythematosus is an immune attack on the body's own tissue — joints, skin, kidneys — affecting roughly 200,000–300,000 people in the US, the large majority of them women.</p>
  <p class="p-src">BioPharma Dive and Alumis, 1 September 2026</p>
</div>

<h2>M&amp;A, licensing and partnerships</h2>

<div class="p-item">
  <p class="p-head">Pfizer → Medicus Pharma — <span class="p-asset">PF-08046031</span><span class="p-tag">Oncology</span></p>
  <p>Pfizer out-licensed a shelved antibody-drug conjugate that came in with Seagen: $12m upfront, $15m on the first anniversary, milestones above $1bn and tiered royalties, with Pfizer contributing $2m toward development and keeping review rights.</p>
  <p>The sums are small; the context is not. This follows a Phase 3 failure for sigvotatug vedotin in lung cancer and the discontinuation of SGN-MesoC2, with an activist investor arguing publicly that Pfizer overpaid for the $43bn acquisition. Each asset moved out is another data point in that argument.</p>
  <p class="p-ind"><b>The disease</b> Melanoma and other solid tumours expressing melanotransferrin. Melanoma accounts for roughly 100,000 new US cases a year.</p>
  <p class="p-src">Fierce Biotech, 3 September 2026; BioSpace, 4 September 2026</p>
</div>

<div class="p-item">
  <p class="p-head">Menarini — <span class="p-asset">bofanglutide, in-licensed from Gan &amp; Lee</span><span class="p-tag">Metabolic</span></p>
  <p>European rights across 39 countries for €62m ($72m) upfront, milestones of up to €664m ($771m) and double-digit royalties. The GLP-1 agonist has completed Phase 3 in China and Phase 2 in the US, with a global Phase 3 planned. The claimed differentiator is dosing once every two weeks rather than weekly.</p>
  <p class="p-flag"><b>Worth checking:</b> the $771m figure is presented as headline deal value in some coverage and as milestones alone in the terms. The two were not reconciled on the pages I could read.</p>
  <p class="p-ind"><b>The disease</b> Type 2 diabetes and obesity. More than 500 million adults worldwide live with diabetes, the large majority of it type 2.</p>
  <p class="p-src">Fierce Biotech, 4 September 2026</p>
</div>

<h2>Development and regulatory</h2>

<div class="p-item">
  <p class="p-head">uniQure — <span class="p-asset">AMT-130 (ifezuntirgene inilparvovec)</span><span class="p-tag">Neurology</span></p>
  <p>A US biologics licence application went in on 2 September under accelerated approval with a priority review request, supported by three-year data showing slowed disease progression. Four-year follow-up follows by month-end, and a European application was filed alongside. Priority review would put a decision roughly eight months out.</p>
  <p>The filing itself is the news because for a year it looked unlikely. The FDA disputed the study design — an external control drawn from a patient database rather than sham surgery — and at one point asked for a new sham-controlled trial, which raised obvious ethical objections. After leadership changes at the agency, the original three-year dataset was accepted as sufficient to file.</p>
  <p class="p-ind"><b>The disease</b> Huntington's is an inherited, fatal degeneration of nerve cells that progressively takes away movement control and cognition, affecting roughly 40,000 people in the US. If approved, this would be the first treatment aimed at the underlying cause rather than the symptoms.</p>
  <p class="p-src">BioPharma Dive and uniQure, 2 September 2026</p>
</div>

<h2>What to watch</h2>

<ul class="p-watch">
  <li><b>uniQure's filing acceptance.</b> The FDA's decision on whether to accept the application, roughly 60 days out, and whether priority review is granted. Those are the binary events — not the submission.</li>
  <li><b>Alumis at the regulator.</b> Whether a subgroup-only Phase 3 in lupus is acceptable, and how a company that just lost half its market value pays for it.</li>
  <li><b>More Seagen divestitures.</b> Plausible given the pressure, but rumoured only — nothing announced.</li>
  <li><b>Conference season.</b> AbbVie's full myeloma dataset lands at the International Myeloma Society meeting this month, ahead of filing.</li>
</ul>
